# Preferred-axis image constraints: initial direct composition

This is a private historical ordinary-file experiment. The frozen percentage-padding compiler rejects every authored constrained request; accepted unconstrained seeds are patched using existing wire properties. There is no public compiler admission or runtime change. This receipt covers only `output/image-preferred-constraints-candidate-r1`, not the separate preferred-axis follow-up campaign.

The image has one percentage preferred dimension, one automatic opposite dimension and its intrinsic aspect ratio. The candidate adds a point or percentage bound on the preferred axis. For the `native-transfer` variant, it omits the automatic-axis minimum and its units to allow native bound-vector ratio completion. For minima and conflicting point bounds, an `explicit-zero-control` variant keeps the automatic minimum explicitly zero. Neither variant adds an automatic-axis maximum or browser-derived dimension.

The 32 recipes cover both preferred axes and both row/column parents: point maximum, point minimum, conflicting point minimum/maximum, percentage maximum and percentage minimum. Minima and conflicts include explicit-zero controls. The source is the original encoded opaque 96×64 image. Parent dimensions are definite percentages; image padding is zero and alignment is flex-start. Source generator, cases, codec, seed compiler, schema and emitted artifacts are bound in `image-preferred-constraints-receipt.json` and the frozen campaign receipts.

All 32 files imported with diagnostic metadata discarded. Their original and cloned scenes resize through 240×240 → 390×320 → 768×560 → 240×240 without recompilation. Across 256 fresh native/Chrome frames, there are 80 geometry passes, 76 pixel passes and 84 image-presence passes. All failures remain at unchanged tolerances.

The eight maximum recipes supply 64 frames: all geometry and presence gates pass, with 60 pixel passes. Four percentage-width maximum frames retain `tail: local RGB error` at the largest viewport, in row and column originals/clones. The other 24 recipes supply 192 frames with only 16 geometry/pixel passes and 20 presence passes. The 16 passing geometry frames are the point minima at the largest viewport, where the authored preferred dimension already exceeds the minimum. They do not qualify active minimum semantics.

Active preferred-axis minima and conflicts visibly clamp the preferred dimension without correctly recomputing the ratio-derived opposite dimension. At 240×240, `width:50%; min-width:200px` produces native 200×80 while Chrome is approximately 200×133.328. Omitting the automatic minimum and preserving explicit zero both exhibit this failure. Width cases are too short; mirrored height cases are too narrow. Conflicting point bounds remain wrong even at the largest viewport. Percentage minima remain active and fail through all measured viewports. Some presence gates pass despite incorrect layout, so presence alone is insufficient.

Direct visual review inspected all 32 original-resolution sheets and 96 representative Chrome/native pairs; 160 exact visual transfers cover all 256 frames. The completed review records visible ratio distortion, inactive-minimum passes and retained maximum-tail failures. Wide Chrome images in small viewports are clipped; the review makes no offscreen-pixel claim. Its receipt is `output/image-preferred-constraints-candidate-r1/visual-r1/review-receipt.json`.

The maxima are a viable finite native candidate, subject to public numerical/API integration and the retained pixel limitations. The minimum failures establish a limitation of these two specific direct compositions, not universal immutable-runtime impossibility. Investigate another ordinary-file composition or source-derived transferred bounds while preserving the original containing percentage basis; bind any follow-up results separately. Image padding, automatic flex minima, genuine cross stretch, nonlegacy flex sizing and broader simultaneous constraints remain outside this initial experiment.
